Real-Time Rendering and Virtual Production

Here are two websites I found helpful while researching this part of animation and visual effects.

♡ Source 1: What is virtual production?

URLhttps://www.unrealengine.com/explainers/virtual-production/what-is-virtual-production?lang=en-US
Site authorEpic Games
AbstractEpic Games’ explainer introduces virtual production as a combination of physical filmmaking and interactive digital technology. It distinguishes several related practices, including previsualization, virtual scouting, live compositing, and in-camera visual effects. The page explains how real-time game engines can help directors and artists preview scenes, plan shots, and work with digital environments before or during filming. It also describes LED stages, where virtual backgrounds appear behind performers and can respond to the movement of a real camera. This resource is important to my research because it shows that animation technology now influences the entire filmmaking process rather than only postproduction. The explanations help connect real-time rendering with practical creative decisions such as lighting, camera placement, and planning. Its examples also show why the term virtual production includes several methods rather than one specific piece of equipment.

♡ Source 2: In-Camera VFX Overview

URLhttps://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/in-camera-vfx-overview-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US
Site authorEpic Games
AbstractThis Epic Games technical overview explains the components required for in-camera visual effects using an LED stage. It describes how live camera tracking, LED displays, and real-time rendering must work together to align digital backgrounds with physical actors and camera movements. The document also identifies Unreal Engine systems that support the process, making it more technical than a general introduction to virtual production. For my research, this source helps explain why filming in front of a digital environment is not as simple as displaying a video on a large screen. The virtual scene must respond accurately to the camera, and the equipment needs careful coordination. It is particularly useful for discussing both the possibilities and limitations of real-time workflows. Because it is official developer documentation, it gives a practical view of the production requirements behind the finished visual effect.
