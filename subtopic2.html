Visual Effects and Digital Compositing

Here are two websites I found helpful while researching this part of animation and visual effects.

♡ Source 1: Nuke compositing software

URLhttps://www.foundry.com/products/nuke-family/nuke
Site authorFoundry
AbstractFoundry’s official Nuke page describes software used to combine and modify visual elements in film and television production. The page emphasizes node-based compositing, which organizes image operations into connected steps rather than relying only on a simple stack of layers. It also identifies an integrated three-dimensional environment and options for extending workflows with programming. These capabilities are useful when an artist needs to combine photographed footage, rendered characters, shadows, backgrounds, and other effects into a unified image. For my research, this page demonstrates why compositing is a specialized stage of visual effects production rather than a minor finishing step. It also helps explain how large projects manage complex adjustments and repeated revisions. Because the information comes from Nuke’s developer, it provides a direct overview of the software’s intended professional use, although its promotional descriptions should be read critically.

♡ Source 2: Explore After Effects features

URLhttps://www.adobe.com/products/aftereffects/features.html
Site authorAdobe
AbstractAdobe’s After Effects features page explains a broad range of tools used for motion graphics and digital visual effects. It covers keyframe animation, motion tracking, background replacement, compositing, color adjustments, and object removal. The page makes it clear that visual effects work often requires several related techniques: a moving object may need to be tracked, isolated, blended with another image, and then adjusted to match the scene. Its examples are helpful for understanding how a single application can support both animated graphics and the integration of live-action footage with digital elements. This source is useful to my paper because it provides concrete examples of compositing tasks that viewers might otherwise overlook. It also offers an accessible comparison with more specialized software such as Nuke. As an official product resource, it is informative about features but not an independent evaluation of their effectiveness.
