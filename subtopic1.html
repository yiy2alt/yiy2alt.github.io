3D Animation and CGI

Here are two websites I found helpful while researching this part of animation and visual effects.

♡ Source 1: Maya overview

URLhttps://www.autodesk.com/products/maya/overview/
Site authorAutodesk
AbstractThis official Autodesk page introduces Maya as a professional application for creating three-dimensional animation and visual effects. It explains how artists use modeling tools to construct characters, props, and environments and then use rigging and animation tools to create movement. The page also describes effects simulation, materials, lighting, and rendering, showing how these stages fit into a larger production workflow. Its discussion of Bifrost is especially useful because it connects artistic work to procedural techniques for generating complex scenes and effects. For my research, this source helps explain that computer-generated imagery is not produced with one simple action. Instead, it is created through a sequence of specialized tasks that require both artistic decisions and technical knowledge. The page is a strong introductory source because it comes directly from the company that develops Maya and identifies the software’s main production capabilities.

♡ Source 2: Character Animation

URLhttps://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Maya-CharacterAnimation/files/GUID-0D0DCBE5-01BA-4AA2-BC4D-85C3285933AD.htm
Site authorAutodesk
AbstractAutodesk’s character animation documentation focuses on the preparation needed before a digital character can move convincingly. It explains the role of skeletons, joints, skin binding, constraints, and deformers in the rigging process. These components allow a model to bend, stretch, and respond to animation controls without losing its intended shape. The resource also shows that character setup involves much more than simply drawing a model or moving it across the screen. Its examples help readers understand how technical controls affect expressive actions, such as a character handling an object or changing body shape. This source is relevant to my research because rigging connects modeling to performance, which is one of the most important stages of a three-dimensional animation pipeline. It is particularly informative for distinguishing the responsibilities of a character modeler, technical artist, and animator.
